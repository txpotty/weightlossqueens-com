    How the Antiparasitic Protocol Works in Valley Stream

    The protocol starts with your symptoms and possible exposures, not with a pill. Your provider considers which parasites are realistic given your travel, pets, food and water, plus anyone at home with the same symptoms. Testing comes next when it would help. Treatment uses prescription anti-parasitics with established uses, and a follow-up confirms symptoms are gone.

    Who This Protocol Is For

    Adults with symptoms or exposures that raise a fair concern for a parasite are the right fit. The most common case is nighttime anal itching in a home with school-age children, which usually points to pinworm. Others have traveled somewhere intestinal worms are common or already have a positive test from another provider. It is not a detox. Nor is it a weight loss program.

    Dosing and Schedule in Valley Stream

    The schedule depends on the drug and the parasite. For pinworm, a single dose of mebendazole 100 mg or albendazole 400 mg is typical, with a second dose two weeks later for newly hatched worms. Ivermectin is dosed by weight. Most ivermectin plans are a single dose or two days in a row. Your provider writes out exact dates for every dose.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Most courses take one to three days, sometimes with a repeat dose two weeks later. Some people feel a little queasy or tired for a day after the pills. With pinworm, the itching usually eases within a few days, and the remaining irritation can take another week to settle. Week two is the repeat dose. A check or a follow-up test may be suggested about a month after treatment.

    Side Effects and Precautions in Valley Stream

    Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea and headache are the side effects people mention most often. With ivermectin, a temporary rash or itch sometimes appears as the parasites die. Because mebendazole and albendazole stay mostly in the gut at standard doses, short courses rarely cause much trouble. Pregnancy changes things. Several of these drugs are not used during pregnancy, and the provider will ask.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks when symptoms started, where you have traveled in the past year and whether pets or family members are involved. Upload any previous stool test results. Providers also screen for pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver disease and current medications that could interact, such as warfarin with ivermectin. Severe abdominal pain, blood in the stool or a high fever mean in-person care. Some people are asked to finish testing before any medication is prescribed.

    How the Protocol Compares to Over-the-Counter Cleanses in Valley Stream
