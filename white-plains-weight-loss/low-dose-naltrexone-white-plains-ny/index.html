    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works for Patients in White Plains, NY

    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works in White Plains

    Standard naltrexone is a 50 mg tablet, FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder, and it blocks opioid receptors for most of the day. LDN uses roughly a tenth of that, usually 1.5 to 4.5 mg at bedtime. The block lasts a few hours. Researchers think the short blockade may lead the body to turn up its own endorphin signaling once the drug wears off, though that idea is still being tested. LDN also seems to quiet microglia, immune cells in the brain and spinal cord linked to pain and inflammation. That is the theory, not settled science.

    Who Low Dose Naltrexone Is For

    Most people who ask about LDN live with fibromyalgia or a long-running inflammatory condition that has not responded well to the usual treatments. The most quoted evidence comes from small trials in fibromyalgia and Crohn's disease. Some patients also ask about it alongside a weight management plan, though it is not a weight loss drug. Anyone taking opioids is out. The provider wants to know what you hope LDN will change and compares that against what the published studies looked at, which is usually pain and fatigue.

    LDN Dosing and Schedule in White Plains

    A common start is 1.5 mg at bedtime, in a compounded capsule or a liquid you measure with an oral syringe. Every one to two weeks the dose usually steps up by about 1.5 mg until it reaches 3 to 4.5 mg. Night dosing lines the short receptor block up with sleep. Vivid dreams can follow. Patients who sleep badly on it are sometimes moved to a morning dose instead, and plenty of them stay there once it works.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Weeks one and two are mostly about sleep. Vivid or odd dreams are the classic early effect, and some people sleep lightly for a few nights after each dose increase. By weeks three and four most patients are near their target dose. The people who benefit tend to notice it slowly, somewhere between 4 and 12 weeks, as fewer flare days or a bit more energy, not as a sudden shift. Check-ins happen at each dose step. Around month three, you and the provider decide whether it has earned a place in your routine.

    Side Effects of LDN in White Plains

    Vivid dreams and insomnia top the list of side effects in published studies, with headache and mild nausea showing up a little less often. These usually ease within a week or two of starting or increasing. The bigger issue is opioids. LDN can block opioid pain medicine from working and can push someone who takes opioids regularly into withdrawal, so even a single tramadol tablet needs to be mentioned. Report dark urine or yellowing skin through secure messaging the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake form asks for every prescription and supplement you take, with a specific question about tramadol and codeine cough syrup because people forget those count as opioids. Liver history matters too. If you have active liver disease or are pregnant, LDN is usually off the table. Nothing is prescribed unless the provider thinks the likely benefit beats the risk in your case. Some patients are asked to upload a recent liver panel showing ALT and AST values.

    How LDN Compares to Related Options in White Plains

    Full-dose naltrexone at 50 mg is FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder and blocks receptors around the clock, which is a different effect from the short nightly block of LDN. Naltrexone gum is another compounded format, newer and with even less published data. On weight loss, GLP-1 drugs win easily. Semaglutide averaged about 15 percent weight loss in the STEP trials, while LDN has no large weight loss trials at all. Your provider can check whether LDN can sit alongside a GLP-1 or another program you already use.
