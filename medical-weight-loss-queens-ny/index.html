    How Medical Weight Loss Works in Queens, NY

    How Medical Weight Loss Works in Queens

    Medical weight loss treats excess weight as a chronic condition. Your provider looks for causes like insulin resistance, untreated sleep apnea, thyroid problems or medicines known to add weight before building a plan. Medication may or may not be part of it. GLP-1 medicines like semaglutide and tirzepatide are common tools because they quiet appetite and help you feel full sooner.

    Who Medical Weight Loss Is For

    Adults who have not reached a healthy weight with diet and exercise alone are the usual fit. Medication is typically an option at a BMI of 30 or above, or 27 or above with a condition tied to weight, such as sleep apnea, high cholesterol or type 2 diabetes. Lower BMIs can still get guidance. Medication is not used during pregnancy.

    Treatment Schedule in Queens

    Week one is the intake and review. If a GLP-1 injection is prescribed, it is taken once weekly with a dose that rises every four weeks, and if an oral medicine is chosen, it is usually taken every morning. Check-ins come every few weeks early on. Once you are stable, check-ins usually space out to every one to three months.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    The first two weeks focus on habits and, if prescribed, a starting dose. Many people on GLP-1 medicine notice less hunger within the first week or two, and mild stomach symptoms show up most after starting and after each dose increase. Expect slow, steady change. Your provider will set a realistic weekly target, often around 1 to 2 pounds.

    Side Effects in Queens

    Side effects depend on the treatment. GLP-1 medicines commonly cause nausea, diarrhea, constipation and reduced appetite, while oral appetite medicines can cause dry mouth, trouble sleeping or a faster heart rate. Fast loss raises gallstone risk. Your provider will push protein and strength training to limit muscle loss.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening covers your BMI, conditions, medications and weight history. A family history of medullary thyroid cancer, uncontrolled blood pressure, an eating disorder or pregnancy each rule out specific medications. Recent bloodwork helps. Your provider may want an A1C, cholesterol, TSH or kidney function results.

    Medical Weight Loss Compared With Other Approaches in Queens

    Commercial diet programs cannot prescribe. They focus on food and behavior, while medical weight loss adds a licensed provider who can treat appetite hormones, manage related conditions and adjust medications that may be adding weight. Treatment options include FDA-approved injections, oral medicines and compounded versions that lack FDA approval. Bariatric surgery fits some higher BMIs.
